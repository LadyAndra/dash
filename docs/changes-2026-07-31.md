## 1. The arrow menu above the keyboard — not changed, and here's why

That menu isn't part of Dash. I searched the whole codebase and there's no floating toolbar in it anywhere.

It's Safari's own **keyboard accessory bar** — the strip iOS puts above the keyboard whenever you're typing in a text field. The `‹ ›` arrows jump between fields and "Done" closes the keyboard. It's drawn by iOS outside the web page, so a website can't remove or restyle it. (An app installed from the App Store could; a web app can't.)

You said to leave it for now, so I have. If it keeps getting in the way, the fix I'd reach for is making the entry panel keyboard-aware — shrinking it to the space the keyboard leaves and scrolling the field you're typing in up above the bar. Say the word.

## 2. Sketch area — now safe to scroll over

**Worth knowing first:** the sketch canvas was *already* always visible in every entry — it's not behind a reveal or a dropdown. If it's been appearing hidden for you, you may be on an older upload. Either way, nothing needed changing there.

The real problem was the scrolling, and I found the cause: the canvas was told to never scroll, and any finger drag across it counted as a pen stroke. It had no way to tell "I'm scrolling past this" from "I'm drawing."

So the paper now has two states, and you pick which one you're in:

- **Viewing** (how it always starts) — the canvas ignores your finger completely. Scroll straight past it like any other part of the page. You can still see the drawing; you just can't add to it. The pen and eraser buttons are greyed out.
- **Drawing** — tap the **✎ Draw** button in the sketch toolbar. The paper gets a dark outline so you can see at a glance that it's live, the button changes to "✎ Drawing on", and now your finger or Pencil makes ink.

Tap the button again to go back to viewing and normal scrolling.

There's a short line of text next to the button that always says which state you're in, so you never have to poke the paper to find out.

**How to try it:** open any entry, scroll down to the Sketch field, and drag your finger across the paper — the page should scroll and leave no mark. Tap **✎ Draw**, drag again — now it draws. Tap it once more and check that scrolling works again.

Undo and Clear stay available in both states, deliberately: taking back a stroke shouldn't require turning drawing on first.

## 3. Multi-select — pick several entries, change them all at once

A **☑ Select** button now sits in the top bar on List, Board, Kanban, Columns, and Project. (Not on Home — there's no list to pick from there.) It's a visible button, not a long-press.

**How it works:**

1. Tap **☑ Select**. A bar appears along the bottom.
2. Tap entries to pick them. Each grows a checkbox and a dark outline, and the bar keeps a running count. Tap a picked entry again to unpick it.
3. Choose an action from the bottom bar:
   - **＋ Tags** — type new tags (Enter after each), or tap any tag you already use. Adds them to everything selected; nothing already there is removed.
   - **Status** — pick one, e.g. On hold. Applies to everything selected.
   - **Project** — pick a project, or make a new one on the spot. Entries can be in several projects at once, so this adds rather than replaces.
4. Tap **Done** — or the Select button again — to leave. Either way the selection clears.

Your selection survives an action on purpose, so you can tag a batch and then set its status without picking everything over again.

**A few deliberate details:**

- **Nothing is a special "bulk" record.** Tagging eight entries is just the same tag edit you'd do one at a time, run eight times. That means sync and merge already know how to handle it, and there's no second way of doing things that could drift out of step with the first.
- **Dragging is paused on Kanban while you're selecting** — a drag and a tap-to-pick are the same gesture on a touchscreen, and a card quietly changing status mid-selection would be an unpleasant surprise. Drag comes straight back when you leave select mode.
- **Switching views clears the selection**, since the entries you'd picked probably aren't on screen any more.
- **If something can't be changed** — say an entry was deleted on your iPad while it was selected on your Mac — the rest still go through and you get a plain-English banner saying how many worked and how many didn't, with a "Copy details" button. It never fails quietly.

**How to try it:** go to List, tap **☑ Select**, tap three entries, then **Status → On hold**. You should get a green "Set 3 entries to On hold" message and see all three update. Tap **Done** to finish.

## One extra fix I made along the way

Dash used to redraw the whole screen after every single edit. That was fine for one edit at a time, but tagging thirty entries would have triggered thirty full redraws in a row and made the app feel frozen for a second or two. Redraws are now bundled into one per screen refresh. You shouldn't notice any difference for ordinary editing — it just stops bulk actions from stuttering.
